---
name: Service and Campaign mode intent
description: User-defined offering lifecycles, public labels, Campaign priority, and evidence constraints.
---

An ongoing offering is internally evergreen but must always be called “Service” in the interface. The word evergreen must never appear in user-visible copy, accessible names or labels. A time-bound offering is internally campaign and publicly “Campaign.”

**Why:** The user calls this a core product concept and explicitly requires plain-language public labels.

A Service has no end date and never ends: its purpose is sustained visibility and a consistent drip of bookings. A Campaign has a start and deadline, and its intended lifecycle builds toward the close and then finishes.

**Why:** These are the user's business definitions, not classifications inferred from post performance or content type.

**How to apply:** The user has now explicitly requested mode-specific behavior. Existing offerings still default to Service rather than inventing campaign dates; users explicitly choose Campaign and its dates.

Services sustain a varied, ongoing rhythm with no ramp or end date. Their overperformer, conversion-gap, hidden flat-post ladder, and maintenance rules remain governed by the existing evidence gates.

Campaigns build from awareness and interest, through proof and momentum, to pricing, urgency and bookings in the final 14 days. They stop generating new suggestions after the deadline.

During a Campaign's final 14 days, the same business's Services ease toward their minimum rhythm, never disappearing. They return to their usual rhythm after the close. Overlapping Campaigns must not release that priority while another closing Campaign is still active.

A Service with no actual or approved presence in the closing window must still be offered a light slot during that window, not deferred entirely beyond the Campaign. An existing planned Service slot already covers that presence.

**Why:** The user explicitly says Services must never disappear entirely during the Campaign peak; ordinary 30-day spacing alone can otherwise leave the whole 14-day window empty.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested these lifecycle differences and coexistence rules, while retaining all honesty gates and the public labels Service and Campaign.

**How to apply:** Determine Campaign content from its proposed posting date, not just the source post's date. Temporary Campaign priority is not a permanent performance penalty for Services.

Do not show the user the content rhythm or internal posting-frequency rules. Keep that data and behavior internally. “Why this move” should offer a short, useful explanation of the strategy and translate actual measured results into everyday meaning, rather than a generic one-line content-type definition.

**Why:** The user explicitly asked to hide the content rhythm and gave final-two-weeks paid-booking intent as an example of the insight they want.

**How to apply:** Explain why a move helps someone decide or book. Campaign timing is meaningful context; cadence, allocation rules and hidden performance counters are not user-facing insights. Do not invent performance conclusions when the evidence gate is unmet.
