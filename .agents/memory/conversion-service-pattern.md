---
name: Conversion service pattern
description: Product intent for service-level conversion gaps, recovery and the trust-first intervention sequence.
---

Conversion gaps describe the trend across the service's latest three measured posts, not a defect in one post. Views are the denominator; saves are evidence of interest, not a separate booking threshold. Keep business/service isolation and the three-complete-results honesty gate.

**Why:** The user explicitly replaced the earlier per-post 20%-of-views-and-saves rule with a last-three-post booking-to-views pattern, roughly below 1–2%. The chosen interpretation is below 2% overall with at least two low-converting posts, so one poor post cannot produce the service finding.

Only the newest measured post carries the current service icon. Opening it should summarize all three results, without blaming that specific post. Before the gate or after recovery, show no conversion icon or summary.

Offer proof/testimonial first, with explicit Add approval. Remember that approval across reloads. Only after the trust post has run and has recorded performance, if the service still has a gap, offer a discount or referral intervention. Never present both stages together or silently add either post.

Recovery ends the intervention episode, but must not delete calendar posts that the user approved. A later recurrence starts at trust again; an old trust approval must not jump a new episode directly to an offer.

**Why:** The user requested “trust first,” “discount only if trust didn't work,” and “clear everything if the service recovers,” while earlier calendar-consent decisions preserve approved posts.

**How to apply:** Keep the conversion signal and its stages separate from ordinary content-sequence suggestions. Retain explicit zero-versus-unknown metrics, approval history, and recovery/recurrence behavior whenever changing this rule.
